# cg-terminal microstructure and agent governance review

Make the market-data collector responsible for freshness, the round compiler responsible for coverage and scoring, and the execution gateway responsible for authorization and risk. The model reviews a frozen round. It has no order credentials and cannot publish the audit verdict.

The checked-in registry resolves to **231 unique assets, 228 with a configured perpetual pair and three classics**. That count is registry membership rather than proof that a feed currently serves a symbol. The descriptions of 61 assets and PIT-13’s 234 are out of step with the actual array. The scanner derives its list from the registry, then adds legacy assets and may apply an environment restriction. Its liquidation/CVD lane rotates four assets every four minutes, so a sweep over 228 takes at least 228 minutes before legacy symbols, skips or failures. The continuity engine archives BTC-only examples of several heavy series and the JSONL archive does not back the live route reads. The 45-second desk snapshot cadence is also separate from broker quote freshness. ## 2. PIT-16 cold-zone stop and sizing

The companion `pit16_cold_zone.py` is executable with `python pit16_cold_zone.py --example`. It accepts verified book and 3D/7D/30D liquidation snapshots, handles both directions, and returns a `Solution` or a typed `Rejected` error. It includes read-only MT5 pricing callbacks and a USD-linear Delta contract adapter. The solver uses a deterministic corridor check and a discrete search over supported quantity steps.

1. **Normalize the trigger coordinate.** Define `d=+1` for a short and `d=−1` for a long. Project the signal-source prices and coverage into the execution stop-trigger coordinate, accounting for instrument multiplier, time-aligned basis, basis uncertainty and bin width. MT5 protection uses the exit side of the quote, typically ask for shorts and bid for longs. Delta protection uses the configured trigger method. Pass `E_ref` as the trigger-side reference when the proposed entry fills and `E_exec` as its executable order price. Include residual adverse trigger-to-fill divergence in the stressed exit offset. Avoid counting identical spread and basis adjustments twice. Reject an unmapped or stale quote. [MQL5 trading constraints](https://www.mql5.com/files/book/mql5book.pdf) · [Delta bracket orders](https://docs.delta.exchange/#place-bracket-order)
2. **Set the adverse corridor before observing the current spread.** Its horizon is bounded by strategy invalidation, demonstrated feed coverage and the remaining target payoff. Supply a structural anchor such as a sweep extreme or value boundary. Upstream adapters should build whole shelves by joining adjacent same-side bins within a configured distance. Keep a shelf’s full interval, including attached tails under its grouping rule, when its peak strength qualifies. The solver accepts those intervals with `low` and `high`, or uses a supplied half-bin for point rows. Use asks and short-liquidation shelves for a short, bids and long-liquidation shelves for a long. A wall must meet the signed asset-specific threshold and arrive in the qualified wall feed. A shelf qualifies at strength **≥40%**. Preserve a provider’s defined strength if available. Otherwise the reference code uses the window peak of the supplied full snapshot. Missing, expired or insufficiently wide windows veto the solve. Never use a top-N display list as proof that the rest of the corridor is clear.
3. **Clear every qualifying interval intersecting the corridor.** In adverse distance coordinates `x=d(p−E_ref)`, let `x*` be the farthest qualifying outer edge or structural anchor, whichever is farther. With edge price `P*`, start with `b=max(2 ticks, 2 × stressed spread, 0.25 × ATR, 0.0015 × |P*|)`. Round `E_ref + d(x*+b)` outward, taking the ceiling for shorts and floor for longs. Veto a rounded stop outside the horizon, too close to the live trigger or order price, or with less than one tick of checked space beyond it. The 0.15% floor comes from the desk’s clearance band. The ATR/spread multipliers and interval join are initial policy settings. For replay, score stops against out-of-sample stop-and-reversal rates, breakouts through walls and post-trigger tail fills. A wall is a probabilistic condition that can be pulled or exhausted. A passed check means the stop clears the observed shelf geometry.
4. **Size against stressed executable loss.** For `P_exit*=SL+d × adverse_exit_offset`, maximize `q` on the venue lot grid subject to `L(q)=−PnL(E_exec,P_exit*,q)+fees(q)+holding_cost_reserve(q) ≤ min(venue_cap, remaining_risk_budget)` and `margin(q) ≤ spendable_margin`. The $6.00 MT5 and $0.50 Delta maxima from the task override the discretionary exceptions in the checked-in specification for this design. Re-read metadata, capture product fee taxes and commission minima, and price the expected protective exit as a taker unless the exact account and exit qualify for a verified promotion. For MT5 use `order_calc_profit` and `order_calc_margin`, then check the actual request with `order_check`. For Delta use contract-specific PnL, maintenance/liquidation checks and available isolated margin. Use the linear adapter only for a verified USD-linear contract. If the minimum size breaches a gate, skip. If multiple pending entries can fill, reserve their joint risk. Leverage changes margin and the liquidation buffer. It does not reduce stop loss at fixed quantity. Gaps and slippage beyond the modeled stress can exceed the planned loss. [MT5 account-currency valuation](https://www.mql5.com/en/book/advanced/python/python_margin_profit) · [Delta fees](https://www.delta.exchange/fees) · [Existing sizing exceptions](C:/Users/SIGMA/Documents/cg-terminal/desk/CANONICAL_MASTER_SPECIFICATION.md:892)

In the supplied ETH example, the proposed stop was **$1.50 before** the reported **$1,856** wall. If that wall is the limiting point edge and the 0.15% clearance controls, the pre-tick lower bound on the short stop is **$1,858.784**. A hypothetical $0.05 tick rounds it to **$1,858.80** and moves the structural distance from **$16.00** to **$20.30** in the example’s common price coordinate. The executable example uses synthetic ATR, coverage, spread, fees and lot metadata. Computing a live order requires verified current inputs, an updated R multiple and a fresh gateway check. For filled positions, run cold-zone diagnostics in observation mode. Any action that would increase booked stop risk requires the existing ownership and reduction/authorization policy. Never automatically widen an open position’s stop based on the new snapshot.

## 3. PIT-18 venue and spread decision

For MT5, let `s*` be the maximum of the latest usable broker spread and the rolling 95th percentile for the relevant symbol/session, with a required historical sample. Let `D0` be the pre-tick structural stop distance computed with the ATR and shelf buffer before the spread term. **Reject MT5 if either observed spread or stressed spread exceeds 20% of `D0`.** That requires a **5×** buffer and is the binding clause in PIT-18. Start with **≤10%** for automatic eligibility, and require a validated symbol/session profile in the **>10% to 20%** band. The solver enforces the hard veto and leaves the eligibility band to the router. Apply the ratio only after establishing the corridor. Re-check the ratio and net payoff at send time. ## 4. Verified 20-minute turns

**Replace self-rearming model recursion with a durable 1,200-second scheduler.** Align local rounds to a fixed UTC phase and associate each round with the newest eligible, separately versioned 15-minute cloud report. Pin an input cutoff and deadline. On restart, resume the active lease or record a skipped slot. Do not execute a backlog of stale rounds. Enforce one active round with a fenced lease and one committed record per round ID. The protection loop continuously applies breakeven, expiry and owned-order rules independently of the LLM.

| --- | --- |
| 0–3s | Compiler verifies the universe, freezes the local snapshot, evaluates all required cells and hard vetoes, computes Table 7 and seals the signed report. |
| 3–6s | Renderer publishes verified coverage and deterministic verdict. A bounded shortlist contains full evidence, costs, worst veto and the strongest near-miss. |
| 6–23s | Model examines the shortlist and near-miss using round-scoped read-only tools. It returns bounded, structured counterarguments and falsifiers. |
| 23–30s | Validator renders accepted evidence-backed fields. The gateway may revalidate a permitted intent with a fresh execution snapshot. On deadline or missing commentary, publish the verified report with commentary timed out and allow the independent protection loop to continue. |

**Use four review lenses and score their evidence.** Require each response to name a claim, two relevant evidence IDs when available, the strongest counterexample, a measurable falsifier and the change it would make to the decision. For market structure, ask what price acceptance contradicts the proposed setup. For opposing flow, ask what spot/perp divergence or wall pull invalidates it. For execution, ask whether stressed spread, slippage and fees erase the payoff at minimum size. For risk, ask what other exposure or feed gap makes the plan infeasible. Stage a competing interpretation and resolve it against the signed evidence with PASS, VETO or INDETERMINATE reasoning. The same model’s four outputs are review lenses with correlated errors. The engine alone applies numerical gates.

Apply GRAVITAS through enforceable verification before claims and mutations. Apply the Karpathy principles by keeping the prompt small, making assumptions explicit and evaluating outcomes with replayable tests. A production prompt can be this short.
